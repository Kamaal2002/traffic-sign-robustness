# Official test findings

Kamaal Alag

Every measured result in this document comes from
`artifacts/analysis/final_test/`, written by `src/compare_test.py` from the
eighteen official test evaluations specified in Part 12 of
`docs/final_test_protocol.md`. The augmentation weights and the plan counts in
section 2 come from `src/augmentation.py` and from enumerating its policy.
The protocol, the decision rule and the clean-accuracy tolerance were frozen
in commit `f16c8a5`
before any test image was classified, and none of them changed after the
results were read. `artifacts/` is not committed, so the tables cited by name
below are regenerated by the commands in `docs/reproduction.md` rather than
read from a clone.

## 1. Research question

Two questions, in order:

1. How much does training-time corruption augmentation reduce the error of a
   standard traffic-sign classifier on corrupted inputs?
2. Does reweighting that augmentation towards the corruption families the
   clean baseline handles worst do better than spreading it uniformly?

The second question is what the experiment is built around, because it changes
one component of an otherwise fixed recipe: the distribution the training
images are drawn from.

The corruptions are synthetic transformations applied to GTSRB images by
`src/corruptions.py` and `src/corruptions_heldout.py`. They stand in for
adverse capture conditions. They are not photographs taken in rain, fog or
night driving, so nothing measured here is a measurement of real
adverse-weather driving performance.

## 2. Experimental design

Three training policies, three seeds each, nine checkpoints in total:

| Arm | Policy | Training distribution |
|---|---|---|
| A | `clean` | the clean recipe, unchanged |
| B | `balanced` | uniform over the six benchmark corruption families |
| C | `targeted` | reweighted towards the families A handles worst |

Arm A at seed 42 is the accepted baseline checkpoint, reused rather than
retrained; the other eight cells were trained for this comparison. Neither
augmented arm is warm-started from the baseline.

B and C augment each training image with probability 0.5, applying exactly one
family at one severity, with severity uniform over levels 1 to 5 in both arms.
C's family weights (occlusion 0.42, gaussian_noise 0.22, fog 0.15,
brightness_bright 0.09, brightness_dark 0.06, gaussian_blur 0.06) come from the
committed baseline robustness curve through a rule fixed before any augmented
training ran; B's weights are 1/6 each.

The two arms are intended to differ only in which family lands on an image, so
that the B-versus-C contrast isolates the family weights. I checked this
against the implementation rather than assuming it.
`AugmentationPolicy.plan` draws in the order `augment`, `severity`, `family`,
`realisation_seed`; the per-image seed is derived from the training seed, the
epoch and the dataset index with no reference to the policy; and both policies
share `AUGMENT_PROBABILITY = 0.5` and the same uniform severity table.
Enumerating 36,000 plans (three training seeds, 30 epochs, 400 image indices)
produced identical augment decisions and identical severities for B and C in
every single plan, with the family differing on 13,826 of the 17,955 augmented
plans. The realisation seed is drawn for every family, including the ones with
no internal randomness, so the occlusion placement that follows does not shift
the earlier draws.

Evaluation is on the official GTSRB test split, 12,630 images, under two
corruption suites kept separate throughout:

* the **original** suite the augmentation was designed against, six families
  at severities 1 to 5 plus clean, 31 conditions;
* a **held-out** suite of five families never sampled during training
  (`contrast_reduction`, `jpeg_compression`, `motion_blur`, `rotation`,
  `salt_pepper`) at severities 1 to 5 plus clean, 26 conditions.

The result files name these two suites `frozen` and `heldout`, which are the
identifiers used throughout the code.

The primary outcome is mean per-image error under corruption: an error
indicator per image on each condition, then an equal-weight mean over that
suite's corrupted conditions, with the clean condition excluded and reported
separately. The contrasts are B−A, C−A and C−B at each seed, negative meaning
the left arm has lower error. Each contrast carries a paired bootstrap over
whole test images, 2,000 resamples, seed 42, percentile interval.

## 3. Primary official test findings

Mean per-image corruption error, averaged over the three training seeds:

| Suite | Arm | Mean | Range across seeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| original | A `clean` | 0.1628 | 0.1550 – 0.1705 |
| original | B `balanced` | 0.0701 | 0.0629 – 0.0827 |
| original | C `targeted` | 0.0657 | 0.0631 – 0.0700 |
| held-out | A `clean` | 0.1372 | 0.1217 – 0.1479 |
| held-out | B `balanced` | 0.0817 | 0.0741 – 0.0915 |
| held-out | C `targeted` | 0.0867 | 0.0821 – 0.0916 |

Both augmented arms cut the clean-trained arm's corruption error by more than
half on the original suite. On the held-out suite the reduction is 40 percent
for B and 37 percent for C.
The seed-to-seed spread is worth noting before reading any small difference:
B's original-suite error ranges from 0.0629 to 0.0827 across three runs that
differ only in seed, a spread larger than the entire B-versus-C difference.

## 4. Balanced augmentation against clean training

B−A is negative at every seed on both suites.

| Suite | Mean | Per seed (42 / 43 / 44) | Seeds favouring B |
|---|---|---|---|
| original | −0.0926 | −0.0921 / −0.0801 / −0.1057 | 3 of 3 |
| held-out | −0.0555 | −0.0737 / −0.0302 / −0.0624 | 3 of 3 |

This is the largest and most consistent effect in the study, and it holds on
the held-out suite of five corruption families that were never sampled during
training, with one family-level exception noted below. On the
original suite the gain concentrates where A was weakest: occlusion −0.2309,
gaussian_noise −0.1700 and fog −0.1198, against brightness_bright −0.0199,
brightness_dark −0.0105 and gaussian_blur −0.0046. It also grows with
severity, from −0.0213 at severity 1 to −0.1856 at severity 5.

One negative result belongs here. On held-out `rotation`, B is slightly worse
than A on the mean, +0.0017, with B lower at two of the three seeds and the
mean carried by a +0.0117 loss at seed 43. C is worse than A on that family at
all three seeds, +0.0080 on the mean. Augmenting with six photometric and
occlusion-style corruptions did not help against a geometric perturbation, and
on that one family it cost a little.

## 5. Targeted augmentation against balanced augmentation

The criterion fixed before the test required C's mean per-image error to be
lower than B's at every one of the three training seeds, on both suites. The
measured result:

| Suite | Mean | Per seed (42 / 43 / 44) | Seeds favouring C |
|---|---|---|---|
| original | −0.0045 | +0.0003 / −0.0128 / −0.0009 | 2 of 3 |
| held-out | +0.0050 | +0.0079 / +0.0002 / +0.0069 | 0 of 3 |

**The criterion is not met on either suite.** On the original suite C is lower
at two seeds of three and marginally higher at seed 42, so the direction is
inconclusive. On the held-out suite C is higher than B at all three seeds. The
verdict recorded in `comparison.json` is "no advantage for the targeted
weights". Per-seed intervals are reported in `paired_contrasts.csv` but take no
part in this decision, which rests on the sign of the point estimate at each
seed.

The family breakdown on the original suite shows what the aggregate hides. Of
the six families, C improves on two and loses on four, measured by across-seed
mean difference:

| Family | C weight | B weight | C−B mean | Seeds C lower |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| occlusion | 0.42 | 0.167 | −0.0380 | 3 of 3 |
| gaussian_noise | 0.22 | 0.167 | −0.0006 | 1 of 3 |
| fog | 0.15 | 0.167 | +0.0022 | 1 of 3 |
| brightness_bright | 0.09 | 0.167 | +0.0042 | 0 of 3 |
| brightness_dark | 0.06 | 0.167 | +0.0033 | 1 of 3 |
| gaussian_blur | 0.06 | 0.167 | +0.0022 | 1 of 3 |

The split follows the weights exactly: the two families C up-weighted relative
to B are the two with a mean improvement, and the four it down-weighted are the
four losses.

Occlusion is the one substantial and seed-consistent improvement, worth 0.0380
on the family C weighted most heavily, with per-seed values of −0.0276, −0.0616
and −0.0249. Gaussian noise shows a mean improvement of only 0.0006 and is not
seed-consistent: C is lower at seed 43 (−0.0097) and higher at the other two
(+0.0027, +0.0051), so the mean is carried by one run. The four losses are each
small, between +0.0022 and +0.0042 on the mean.

Averaged over the six families with equal weight, the occlusion gain is partly
offset by the four smaller losses. What survives is a modest average advantage
for C of 0.0045, which is not consistent across all three seeds: C is lower at
seeds 43 and 44 and marginally higher at seed 42. A modest average advantage
that two of three seeds support is not what the criterion asks for, and it is
also smaller than the seed-to-seed spread of either arm.

The alignment between the weights and the family-level outcomes is an
association observed in this experiment, not an established rule. Six families,
three seeds and two weightings are not enough to separate the weight assigned
to a family from everything else that moves with it: the families are not
independent of one another, a weight raised on one family is a weight removed
from the others, and no intermediate weighting was trained. The equal weighting
across families in the aggregate is also a property of the benchmark rather
than of the models, so a differently weighted suite would score these same
checkpoints differently. That is a reason to read the aggregate as specific to
this suite, not a reason to reweight the suite after seeing the result.

## 6. Generalization to the held-out corruption types

The held-out suite is where the two augmented arms separate most clearly, and
not in C's favour. C is worse than B at all three seeds, and the gap widens
with severity, from +0.0009 at severity 1 to +0.0138 at severity 5. By family,
across-seed means:

| Family | C−B mean | Seeds C lower |
|---|---|---|
| contrast_reduction | +0.0159 | 0 of 3 |
| rotation | +0.0063 | 0 of 3 |
| jpeg_compression | +0.0036 | 1 of 3 |
| motion_blur | +0.0021 | 1 of 3 |
| salt_pepper | −0.0029 | 1 of 3 |

The two families C loses most on are the two least like anything it
up-weighted. Its only held-out improvement is on `salt_pepper`, the family
closest to the gaussian noise it weighted at 0.22, and even that is not
seed-consistent.

Both augmented arms still transfer better than no augmentation at all: B−A is
−0.0555 and C−A is −0.0505 on this suite, each consistent across all three
seeds.

These generalization statements are scoped to the five corruption families in
this suite and to the one architecture tested, the 1.45M-parameter
`BaselineCNN` of `src/model.py`. Both suites were also evaluated on the same
12,630 official test images, and the held-out suite is held out in corruption
type only. It is not an independent population of physical signs, so it
measures transfer across perturbation types, not transfer across sign
instances, cameras or capture conditions.

## 7. Clean-accuracy trade-offs

Clean test accuracy and macro F1 per checkpoint:

| Arm | Seed 42 | Seed 43 | Seed 44 | Mean accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A `clean` | 0.9865 / 0.9820 | 0.9822 / 0.9650 | 0.9773 / 0.9578 | 0.9820 |
| B `balanced` | 0.9865 / 0.9799 | 0.9789 / 0.9664 | 0.9821 / 0.9678 | 0.9825 |
| C `targeted` | 0.9831 / 0.9758 | 0.9831 / 0.9767 | 0.9804 / 0.9659 | 0.9822 |

`CLEAN_TRADEOFF_LIMIT = 0.01` is a reporting rule, not part of the pass or fail
criterion. A clean cost above it means the result is reported as a trade-off
rather than as an improvement; the verdict itself rests on the per-seed
corruption-error directions on both suites and does not read this column. Clean
cost is measured against each contrast's own right-hand arm at the same seed,
so the cost on the C−B row is B's clean accuracy minus C's; that reading and
the conflicting passage in the protocol are recorded in
`docs/final_test_deviations.md`.

Every row of `clean_tradeoff.csv` has `exceeds_clean_limit = False`. The largest
absolute clean cost on any row is 0.0048, and several costs are negative,
meaning the augmented arm was also slightly better on clean images. No result
here had to be reported as a clean-accuracy trade-off.

No arm pays a meaningful clean-accuracy price for augmentation. The three arm
means span 0.00053 (A 0.98197, B 0.98250, C 0.98221), while the seed-to-seed
spread within an arm reaches 0.0092 for A, so this evaluation does not separate
the arms on clean accuracy.

## 8. Validation against official test

`validation_reproduction.csv` places the accepted validation analysis beside
the test result. All six across-seed mean contrast directions agree between
the two splits.

| Suite | Contrast | Validation mean | Test mean | Favours | Seed consistency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| original | B−A | −0.0885 | −0.0926 | B | 3 of 3 → 3 of 3 |
| original | C−A | −0.0919 | −0.0971 | C | 3 of 3 → 3 of 3 |
| original | C−B | −0.0033 | −0.0045 | C on the mean | 3 of 3 → 2 of 3 |
| held-out | B−A | −0.0524 | −0.0555 | B | 3 of 3 → 3 of 3 |
| held-out | C−A | −0.0472 | −0.0505 | C | 3 of 3 → 3 of 3 |
| held-out | C−B | +0.0053 | +0.0050 | B | 0 of 3 → 0 of 3 |

The one row where `validation_reproduction.csv` records
`directions_agree = False` is the original-suite C−B contrast, and the
disagreement is in seed-level consistency rather than in direction. The mean
stayed negative and in fact moved slightly further in C's favour, from −0.0033
to −0.0045, while the count of favourable seeds fell from 3 of 3 to 2 of 3. The
label the comparison assigns therefore changed from "consistent" to
"seed-sensitive", which is what the flag reports.

Reading the six rows by outcome:

* **Reproduced.** Both augmentation-against-clean effects on both suites, in
  direction, in approximate magnitude and at all three seeds. The held-out C−B
  result also reproduced: C was worse than B on unseen corruption types in
  validation and is worse on test, with almost the same mean.
* **Strengthened.** Each augmentation-against-clean effect is slightly larger
  on test than on validation, by 0.0030 to 0.0052.
* **Weakened.** Original-suite C−B, which lost its seed consistency as
  described above. The validation analysis had already flagged this contrast as
  the fragile one; the test split moved it from consistent to inconclusive.
* **Reversed.** None.
* **Uncertain.** Original-suite C−B, whose per-seed estimates straddle zero
  (+0.0003, −0.0128, −0.0009), and the family-level C−B rows for
  `gaussian_noise` and `salt_pepper`, whose signs are not stable across seeds.

Agreement here is agreement between two evaluations of the same nine
checkpoints, on different images, under the same corruption definitions. It
supports the claim that these effects are not an artifact of the validation
split. It does not establish that they hold for other architectures, other
datasets or real adverse-weather capture, none of which this study varied.

## 9. Statistical limitations

* **The intervals assume independent test images.** GTSRB is assembled from
  sequences of about 30 frames per physical sign, so test images are very
  likely clustered by sign. The official test release carries no track
  identifiers, so the dependence cannot be measured from it, and the
  image-level percentile intervals are narrower than a dependence-aware
  interval would be. They are not definitive population-level confidence
  intervals.
* `design_effect_sensitivity.csv` illustrates what a cluster size of 30 at
  perfect within-cluster correlation would imply, a widening by a factor of
  about 5.48. It is an assumption-dependent illustration, not a bound, not a
  corrected interval, and not an input to any decision.
* **No interval entered the decision.** The targeted-augmentation rule is the
  sign of the per-seed point estimate at every seed on both suites, as frozen
  before the test ran.
* **Three seeds give a direction and a spread, not a significance claim.** No
  test across seeds was computed, and seed variability was deliberately not
  pooled with the image-level sampling uncertainty.
* **The two suites are not independent of each other.** Both were evaluated on
  the same 12,630 images.
* **The corruptions are synthetic.** Severity levels are parameter settings in
  `src/corruptions.py` and `src/corruptions_heldout.py`, not calibrated
  physical conditions, so the error rates describe behaviour under those
  transformations and not under real rain, fog, motion or sensor noise.
* **Secondary breakdowns are descriptive.** The family, severity, per-class
  recall and class-balanced tables carry no inference and no decision. All 43
  classes appear in every per-class block, with none selected or dropped.
  Class-balanced error is higher than image-weighted error for every arm, so
  the headline numbers benefit to some degree from the test split's class
  frequencies.

### Class-balanced sensitivity analysis

Official test support runs from 60 to 750 images per class, so the primary
metric does not weight the 43 classes equally. Reweighting them is a direct
sensitivity check on the headline comparison: if the verdict depended on the
test split's class frequencies, that would be a property of GTSRB rather than
of the training policies.

The primary metric averages per-image error within a condition, then averages
conditions with equal weight. The class-balanced metric inserts one step:
within a condition, image errors average inside each class first, and the 43
class means then average with equal weight. Conditions still average equally
and the clean condition is still excluded, so the only change is the weight a
class carries. `class_balanced_error.csv` and `class_balanced_contrasts.csv`
hold the values below, and all 43 classes are present under every condition of
both suites for every arm, with none selected or dropped.

Across-seed mean error, both weightings:

| Suite | Arm | Image-weighted | Class-balanced |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| original | A: clean | 0.1628 | 0.1817 |
| original | B: balanced | 0.0701 | 0.0934 |
| original | C: targeted | 0.0657 | 0.0913 |
| held-out | A: clean | 0.1372 | 0.1615 |
| held-out | B: balanced | 0.0817 | 0.1117 |
| held-out | C: targeted | 0.0867 | 0.1205 |

Class-balanced error is higher in all six arm-suite cells, by 0.019 to 0.034,
so error is concentrated in the classes with fewer test images and the headline
numbers benefit from the test split's frequencies. The arm ordering does not
move: C below B below A on the original suite, B below C below A on the
held-out suite, the same ordering the primary metric gives. Both augmented
arms remain lower than clean training at every seed on both suites, with
across-seed means of −0.0883 and −0.0904 on the original suite and −0.0499 and
−0.0411 on the held-out suite for B−A and C−A.

The targeted-against-balanced contrast is where the weighting matters, and it
changes two of the eighteen per-seed comparisons:

| Suite | Weighting | Seed 42 | Seed 43 | Seed 44 | Mean | C lower |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| original | image-weighted | +0.0003 | −0.0128 | −0.0009 | −0.0045 | 2 of 3 |
| original | class-balanced | +0.0055 | −0.0153 | +0.0037 | −0.0020 | 1 of 3 |
| held-out | image-weighted | +0.0079 | +0.0002 | +0.0069 | +0.0050 | 0 of 3 |
| held-out | class-balanced | +0.0138 | −0.0004 | +0.0129 | +0.0088 | 1 of 3 |

Seed 44 on the original suite flips from C lower to B lower, and seed 43 on the
held-out suite flips the other way. Both are contrasts the primary metric puts
below 0.001 in magnitude, −0.0009 and +0.0002, where a change of weighting is
enough to cross zero. The third contrast that small, +0.0003 at seed 42 on the
original suite, moved further from zero instead. Nothing else changes sign.

The predefined criterion is unaffected. It requires C below B at all three
seeds on both suites, and C reaches one of three on each suite under class
balancing against two of three and none of three under the primary metric. The
targeted policy fails the criterion under either weighting, and on the original
suite its modest average advantage shrinks from 0.0045 to 0.0020 while
disagreeing across seeds more, not less.

This establishes that the reported verdict does not rest on the class
frequencies of the official test split. It does not make the class-balanced
numbers a second result: they carry no interval, no per-class inference and no
decision, they are computed from the same eighteen prediction files rather than
from new evaluations, and equalising class weights does nothing about either of
the two dependence problems recorded above, the frames sharing a physical sign
or the three training seeds.

## 10. Conclusion

Corruption augmentation helped substantially, including on corruption types it
never saw. Uniform augmentation over the six families cut mean corruption error
on the official test split from 0.1628 to 0.0701 on the original suite and from
0.1372 to 0.0817 on the held-out suite, at every one of the three training
seeds, with no meaningful clean-accuracy cost.

The targeted policy tested here did not beat the uniform one. It fails the
criterion fixed before the test: lower than B at two of three seeds on the
original suite and at none of the three on the held-out suite, where it is
consistently worse. On the original suite its occlusion gain is partly offset
by smaller losses on four families, leaving a modest average advantage of
0.0045 that two of the three seeds support. On the held-out suite it transfers
less well than the uniform policy at every seed.

That is a result about this particular weighting, not about augmentation
weighting in general. The weights were derived from the baseline's per-family
accuracy drop, normalised with a 5 percent floor, and applied at a fixed
augmentation probability of 0.5 with severity held uniform. A different
derivation, a milder reweighting, or a policy that added capacity on the
weakest family without removing it from the others was not tested and is not
ruled out by these results. What this experiment does show is that the large
and seed-consistent gain came from augmenting with corruptions at all, and that
concentrating the same augmentation budget on the baseline's worst family did
not produce a reliable net gain over spreading it evenly.
